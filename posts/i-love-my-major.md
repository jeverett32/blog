I just finished my first semester in the Information Systems major at BYU, and to sum it up: it has been going really good.

At first, I was unsure how much I would actually like the program. Obviously I am obsessed with building apps, using AI, and working with data, so it makes sense on paper. However, I didn’t anticipate how much the program would actually teach me. I learned things that I never thought I would be so passionate about.

I was sort of just expecting some normal classes where I would learn a couple of things and half-ass my way to a good grade while I worked on things I actually liked. I was lowkey just expecting my classes to suck. Since it was recruiting season for internships, I assumed I would just be trying to develop the skills I needed for the jobs I wanted while grinding away on applications. I was pretty surprised when the internship I ended up getting asked me to do a case study. I had to create a data science notebook to explore a dataset, create regression models, and make a business decision based on my findings. It was something I had an entire class about. Who knew they actually thought about what they were teaching us?

### The Semester Breakdown

I was overall impressed by the content I learned this semester. Here is the lineup:

* IS 402: Database normalization, creation, and querying with SQL.
* IS 403: Web Development using JavaScript, Node JS, and EJS.
* IS 404: Learning how the internet works, including the TCP/IP Model layers, deploying web apps on AWS, Security Certificates, and DNS records.
* IS 415: Data analytics. We used pandas in data science notebooks for exploratory data analysis, made charts, and created dashboards in Tableau and Power BI.

My database (402) and data communications (404) classes were definitely my favorites to start. The content felt relevant and useful. Learning how the internet actually works fascinated me because it felt completely new. Learning SQL was also useful for my job, which probably helped. Plus, my professors for those classes were absolute nerds and I loved it.

My data analytics class had interesting concepts, and I knew they were important, but it took me a while to get into it. Learning the syntax of pandas made me want to die inside and take everyone within a mile radius with me.

### Priorities and "Locking In"

I spent most of the semester not caring that much. I actually pride myself on having a life and I was busy living it. Between working so I can eat, looking for an internship so I can do something with my life, playing way too much volleyball than is good for a person, and trying to get a girl to love me, school was pretty low on my list of priorities. I’ve also gotten so good at doing the bare minimum to get a good grade. I have kind of mastered it. I don’t know how I have a 4.0 because it is insane.

Anyway, I kind of went in and out of being "locked in" for my classes. I started off super strong. I was reading the textbooks, doing the homework, and studying for tests. Then I said screw it after I got an internship and my first girlfriend. I started only caring enough to know what was going on so I could take the exams and be fine. Then I got dumped after eight days and locked in again.

### INTEX (Integrated Experience)

The peak of the semester was definitely our INTEX project. INTEX is where the professors take everything they’ve taught you, get together, drink a bunch of alcohol, and come up with the most devious assignment their drunk and evil minds can come up with. Except they don't drink, so they’re just evil. This project is so big that they cancel all classes for a week and force you to take off work by blackmailing you. Luckily (or maybe unluckily for some people), you get a group to work with! It is a great trauma bonding experience.

To be honest though, the project wasn't that bad. We came up with a pretty good product without too much stress, and we had a lot of fun doing it. I was just happy to not have to wake up and see everyone’s ugly 8:00 AM faces in class. They were probably more happy not to see mine though.

### Final Thoughts

Back to my thoughts on the IS program so far. I’ve already talked it up a lot because it teaches awesome skills I actually use. However, my FAVORITE part is that it is so easy. Even though it was a lot of work sometimes, I had so much time to work on things on my own.

One of the interesting parts of the IS program is that you get out what you put in. They give you the time to focus on what you want. They give you the time to go out and get real work experience, attend events, and network. I spent so much time just working on personal projects that make me look good and teach me real skills. It is incredible and perfect for the type of education I was looking for.

To sum it up, Information Systems is giving me good skills, but more importantly, it is giving me FREEDOM. I have grown so much in the last semester, and I accredit a lot of that to my major.
